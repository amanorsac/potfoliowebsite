# Before / after audio

The clips behind the Before/After players on the mixing page go here.
A file in this folder is reachable at:

    /audio/whatever-you-called-it.mp3

## How it works on the page

Three players, each with a Before and an After button. The toggle keeps
your place in the passage, so a listener compares the same moment, not
two different bars. The page currently expects these six files:

    drums-raw.mp3      /  drums-mixed.mp3      drums
    lead-raw.mp3       /  lead-mixed.mp3       lead vocal
    backing-raw.mp3    /  backing-mixed.mp3    backing vocals

Those slots are not fixed. If what you have is two different pairs, or
four, say so - the titles, notes and count all live in one list and take
a minute to change.

## What to send

**Pairs.** Every clip needs its other half, and both halves must be the
SAME passage - same bars, same take. A before from verse one and an
after from the chorus proves nothing.

**15-20 seconds each.** Long enough to hear the room, short enough that
somebody actually listens to both.

**WAV straight out of the session is best.** Full quality, unmastered
export; I convert down to what the page serves the same way the gallery
photographs were converted. MP3 is fine too if that is what exists.

**Level-match the pair by ear before exporting.** If the after is simply
louder, the comparison proves nothing and an experienced listener will
notice in the first two seconds. This is the one thing I cannot fix from
here - loudness decisions are yours.

Then tell me which file is the before and which the after of each pair,
and one line on what the pair shows:

    choir-desk.wav / choir-release.wav - board feed against the
    mixed and mastered release, Sunday service

I wire them into the players and adjust the titles to match what the
clips actually are.

## Two things worth checking before sending

The excerpts go on a public page: make sure the artist is happy with
that, in the same way the artist chips went up with their blessing.

And pick passages that make the point on phone speakers, because that is
where most visitors will hear them - a low-end clean-up that only shows
on monitors is real work that this page cannot demonstrate.
